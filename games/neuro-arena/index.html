    How Neuro Arena works

    A carousel of contests. Pick the game at the top (arrows, swipe, click, or the [ and ] keys): Pong, Addition, Subtraction, Bigger number, Even or odd, Text completion, Rock paper scissors, Echo, Count the dots, Pattern completion and Simon. Every contest has its own evolution, champion, Hall of Fame and history, saved in this browser. In the quiz contests the problem is shown to both brains at once through symbol input neurons (one tile per symbol, read by a gain-field association layer), and each possible answer has its own motor population. Every trial runs the same loop, shown by the bar under the title: PRESENT (the problem drives the input neurons for 40 ms), THINKING (300 ms in which the answer populations integrate evidence; nothing is read early), ANSWER (winner-take-all on the spike counts of the whole window; the brain commits to the population that fired most, and only against you in Play does it abstain when its lead is below a confidence threshold) and JUDGED (THE REAPER scores the answer, dopamine is released only then: positive for right, negative for wrong, small for abstaining, and it reinforces the synapses that were active during that trial). Then activity is reset to baseline before the next problem. Addition and Subtraction use a curriculum: they start at 0–2 + 0–2 and the operand range widens each time the brain gets 90% right (the level is shown on screen and marked on the Lab curve). Even or odd uses numbers up to 999, coded by digit place (a ones-digit tile and a tens-digit tile), so the brain must discover that only the ones digit matters.

    THE REAPER. Selection is decided by a judge, not by the live duel. After each duel the champion and its spawn sit the same frozen benchmark (learning off, the same items in the same order). Each answer is scored per character: +1 for a right character, −2 for a wrong one, 0 for abstaining, so guessing costs more than staying silent. The challenger reaps the champion only if it leads by a clear margin (5% of the maximum, at least 3 points; Pong: 2 serves) and wins on more items than it loses (a paired sign test). Ties go to fewer errors, then to faster answers. The verdict appears on screen and in the Lab.

    Two brains, one game. Each player is its own brain of spiking neurons: 2,800 in Pong; in the quiz contests the Brain size slider sets 1,400 to 22,400 (default 2,800), with the same physiology as Neuro Storm. Cells are conductance-based integrate-and-fire neurons. They rest at −70 mV, fire past about −55 mV, spike to +35 mV, and undershoot to about −79 mV, with a 2 ms absolute refractory period and a relative one. AMPA EPSPs reverse at 0 mV and GABA-A IPSPs at −80 mV. 80% of cells are excitatory and 20% inhibitory, and Dale's law holds.

    Sensing → acting. Receptors encode where the ball is relative to the paddle (a foveal code, magnified near the paddle, like an eye fixating it), the paddle's height, the ball's distance and its vertical velocity as population codes, sending Poisson spikes into a thalamic relay with TRN inhibition. From there activity goes to sensory cortex, then to an E/I-balanced association cortex, where gain-field cells respond to particular (ball − paddle) × paddle-height combinations, and then to motor layer 5, which has an UP group and a DOWN group. Lateral inhibition makes the two groups compete, and the paddle moves according to the UP vs DOWN firing rate. Nothing else moves the paddle.

    Learning within a lifetime. Association → motor synapses learn by reward-modulated STDP, a three-factor rule. Pre/post spike timing marks each synapse with an eligibility trace, and dopamine turns those traces into weight changes. Only pre/post activity above each motor cell's baseline counts, so random firing does not drift the weights. A hit or a point gives a dopamine burst, and a miss gives a dip, but those arrive too late to credit the movement that caused them; an innate critic (moving toward the ball → a small burst every 5 ms, moving away → a dip) is what does most of the teaching. Homeostatic synaptic scaling keeps motor firing near a target rate.

    Evolution across lifetimes. The genome holds 49 genes: connection probabilities and weights, E/I balance, learning rates, time constants, receptive-field width, motion anticipation, innate motor biases, and structural switches. The reigning champion duels its newest spawns. The duel is the show and a lifetime of learning; then both brains, with learning frozen, take the same fixed benchmark of 50 standard serves. A spawn takes the throne only if it is clearly better there (by 2+ serves and a paired sign test), so lucky duels do not count. The champion keeps its best-scoring synapses, so its benchmark never goes down. The winner reproduces. Each spawn is a copy with a small copy-error rate, like DNA replication errors. Most errors are small, some are large point errors, and a few are structural. Darwinian inheritance passes on genes only, so every child relearns from scratch. Lamarckian inheritance also passes on the learned synapses, with copy errors. The Hall of Fame keeps the best champions, and an old champion can return for a rematch.

    Modes. Watch shows each generation's duel live. Turbo runs many duels (and benchmarks) at once in background workers. Play lets you face the current champion; it keeps learning while you play.

    The Lab (LAB button or L) is for studying how the brains teach themselves: benchmark accuracy against chance over generations, live accuracy in the current duel, the dopamine (reward prediction error) trace, the champion's synapse histogram and how far its synapses drift each generation, a spike raster with firing rates per area, the lineage with the effect of each kind of copy error, every Reaper verdict, and Lamarckian vs Darwinian results. Export CSV or JSON for your own analysis.
