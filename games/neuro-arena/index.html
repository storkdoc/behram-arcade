    How Neuro Arena works

    Two brains, one game. Each paddle is driven by its own brain of 2,800 spiking neurons, with the same physiology as Neuro Storm. Cells are conductance-based integrate-and-fire neurons. They rest at −70 mV, fire past about −55 mV, spike to +35 mV, and undershoot to about −79 mV, with a 2 ms absolute refractory period and a relative one. AMPA EPSPs reverse at 0 mV and GABA-A IPSPs at −80 mV. 80% of cells are excitatory and 20% inhibitory, and Dale's law holds.

    Sensing → acting. Receptors encode the ball's height, the paddle's height, the ball's distance and its vertical velocity as population codes, sending Poisson spikes into a thalamic relay with TRN inhibition. From there activity goes to sensory cortex, then to an E/I-balanced association cortex, where gain-field cells respond to particular ball × paddle combinations, and then to motor layer 5, which has an UP group and a DOWN group. Lateral inhibition makes the two groups compete, and the paddle moves according to the UP vs DOWN firing rate. Nothing else moves the paddle.

    Learning within a lifetime. Association → motor synapses learn by reward-modulated STDP, a three-factor rule. Pre/post spike timing marks each synapse with an eligibility trace, and dopamine turns those traces into weight changes. A hit or a point gives a dopamine burst, and a miss gives a dip. An innate critic adds small phasic signals for moving toward the ball. Homeostatic synaptic scaling keeps motor firing near a target rate.

    Evolution across lifetimes. The genome holds 49 genes: connection probabilities and weights, E/I balance, learning rates, time constants, receptive-field width, motion anticipation, innate motor biases, and structural switches. The reigning champion duels its newest spawn, and the winner reproduces. Each spawn is a copy with a small copy-error rate, like DNA replication errors. Most errors are small, some are large point errors, and a few are structural. Darwinian inheritance passes on genes only, so every child relearns from scratch. Lamarckian inheritance also passes on the learned synapses, with copy errors. The Hall of Fame keeps the best champions, and an old champion can return for a rematch.

    Modes. Watch shows each generation's duel live. Turbo runs many duels at once in background workers. Play lets you face the current champion; it keeps learning while you play.
